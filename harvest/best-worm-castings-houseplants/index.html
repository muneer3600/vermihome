The best worm castings for most houseplants are Wiggle Worm 100% Pure Organic Worm Castings in the 15 lb bag. Worm castings are the finished product of a worm bin: dark, crumbly, earthy-smelling material that feeds plants slowly and improves potting soil. You can make your own — see how to harvest worm castings — but most houseplant owners buy a bag. This guide compares six options by the things that matter indoors: purity, price per pound, bag size, and whether the product is actually castings or mostly filler.

1. Wiggle Worm 15 lb — best overall for houseplants

Wiggle Worm is the most widely sold castings brand in the US, and the 15 lb bag is its sweet spot for houseplant owners. Farmstand’s October 2026 roundup listed the 15 lb bag at $26.75 and the 30 lb bag at $36.04, which makes the bigger bag cheaper per pound — but only if you will use 30 lb before it goes stale. For most people with a dozen houseplants, 15 lb lasts a year or more.

The selling point is purity. Wiggle Worm says its product is 100% pure castings, OMRI-listed, and made with a manufacturing process that separates castings from uneaten bedding. That matters because cheap “worm castings” are often mostly bedding with a little castings mixed in — you pay for weight that does nothing. The castings are described as tiny, football-shaped particles that improve aeration and drainage, and the NPK is listed as 1-0-0, which is typical for pure castings: gentle, slow-release, and hard to over-apply.

Best for: most houseplant owners. Buy the 15 lb bag if you have 10+ plants; buy the 4.5 lb bag if you have fewer.

2. Wiggle Worm 30 lb — best value per pound

If you also feed a vegetable garden or a large indoor collection, the 30 lb bag at $36.04 is about $1.20 per pound — the cheapest pure castings in this guide. The catch is storage: castings should stay dry and covered, and an open 30 lb bag in a damp apartment can clump. If you can store it in a sealed bin, this is the best value. If not, the 15 lb bag is the smarter buy.

One warning: prices for the same bag vary wildly by seller. Greenhouse Megastore listed the 15 lb bag at $13.99 and the 30 lb bag at $22.99, well below the Amazon prices above. That may be a different listing, an older price, or a sale — check the seller before you assume either price is the real one.

Best for: houseplant owners who also garden outdoors, or anyone splitting a bag with a friend.

3. Wiggle Worm 4.5 lb — best small bag

The 4.5 lb bag costs about $18.71, which is roughly $4.16 per pound — more than twice the per-pound price of the 15 lb bag. You are paying for the small size. But for someone with three or four houseplants, 4.5 lb is genuinely enough for a year of top-dressing, and you are not storing a heavy bag you will never finish. It is also the easiest bag to carry home from a garden centre.

Best for: beginners, small collections, and anyone who wants to try castings before committing to a big bag.

4. Uncle Jim’s Black Gold 4 lb — best premium small bag

Uncle Jim’s Worm Farm has sold worms and castings for over two decades. Its Black Gold castings are screened to 3/8-inch consistency and OMRI-certified, and Farmstand listed the 4 lb bag at $25.95 — about $6.49 per pound, the most expensive per pound in this guide.

Why would anyone pay that? Consistency and trust. Uncle Jim’s is a worm farm first, not a repackager, and its castings come from its own worms. If you have a few prized plants and want the most consistent product, this is a fair choice. If you have twenty plants, the price per pound will hurt.

Best for: small collections of valuable plants, and buyers who prefer to buy from a worm farm directly.

5. Brothers Worm Farm 10 lb — best from a worm farm

Brothers Worm Farm sells small-batch castings in 5, 10, and 20 lb sizes, packed in a reusable breathable cotton bag. The 10 lb bag costs $33.00 with free shipping — $3.30 per pound. The castings are fine and sifted, which matters for houseplants: coarse castings sit on top of potting soil and dry out, while fine castings mix in evenly.

Brothers also sells worms — see where to buy red wigglers online — so if you are already ordering worms, adding a bag of castings to the same order saves a second shipping fee.

Best for: buyers who want small-batch castings from a specialist worm farm, especially if they are already ordering worms.

6. Back to the Roots 5 lb — best for versatility, price unverified

Back to the Roots Organic Worm Castings in the 5 lb bag is the best-overall pick in VarietyChem’s 2026 roundup, praised for consistent quality and a balanced nutrient profile. It is made in the US and sized for both indoor and outdoor use.

We could not verify a current price for the 5 lb bag on 6 November 2026. Amazon listings for it exist, but the search results we checked showed no stable price. Do not trust a price from an old roundup — check the listing on the day you buy. If it is priced near $15–$20, it is a fair alternative to the Wiggle Worm 4.5 lb bag. If it is priced near $30, buy the Wiggle Worm 15 lb bag instead and get three times the castings.

Best for: buyers who find it at a fair price. Not a first choice at an unknown price.

How much castings should you use on houseplants?

A little goes a long way. For top-dressing, sprinkle about a quarter inch of castings on the soil surface every two to three months, then water it in. For repotting, mix roughly one part castings to four parts potting soil. Castings are gentle — they will not burn roots the way synthetic fertilizer can — but more is not better. A thick layer on top of the soil can crust over and block water.

If you make your own castings, the same amounts apply. Our guide to how to use worm castings for houseplants covers tea, top-dressing, and repotting mixes in detail, and worm castings vs compost explains when castings are worth the price and when ordinary compost will do.

What to check before you buy

  • Purity. Look for “100% pure worm castings” and an OMRI listing. Avoid products that list “vermicompost” with no purity claim — they may be mostly bedding.
  • Price per pound. Always divide the price by the bag weight. A $36 bag of 30 lb is cheaper than a $19 bag of 4.5 lb, even though the second number looks smaller.
  • Bag size vs your plants. A 30 lb bag is a bargain only if you use it. For fewer than ten houseplants, a 4.5–5 lb bag is enough for a year.
  • Smell. Real castings smell like fresh earth. If a bag smells sour or like ammonia, return it.

FAQ

Are worm castings better than liquid fertilizer for houseplants? For most houseplants, yes as a base feed — castings release nutrients slowly, improve soil structure, and are hard to over-apply. Liquid fertilizer works faster for a plant that is actively growing and hungry. Many owners use both: castings every few months, liquid feed in the growing season.

How often should I add worm castings to houseplants? Every two to three months as a top-dressing, or at repotting time mixed into the soil. More often is not harmful, but it is wasteful.

Can I use worm castings on all houseplants? Yes, with one caution: very sensitive plants such as orchids and carnivorous plants do not want rich soil at all. For ordinary houseplants — pothos, monstera, snake plants, ferns — castings are safe.

Is it cheaper to make my own castings? Yes, after the first few months. A worm bin costs $65–$140 to buy (see best worm bin for apartments) and then produces castings from kitchen scraps for years. If you only need one bag a year, buying is cheaper. If you feed castings to a whole collection, a bin pays for itself.
