How Many Worms Do I Need? Simple Starter Math

Published October 15, 20265 min read

Start with half a pound to one pound of red wigglers — roughly 500 to 1,000 worms. A composting worm eats about half its body weight per day, so a pound of worms handles roughly half a pound of food scraps daily. Once your bin is established, the population grows on its own to match how much you feed.

The half-body-weight rule

This is the only math you need. A composting worm eats roughly half its body weight in food scraps every day. So:

  • 1 lb of worms → about 1/2 lb of scraps per day
  • 1/2 lb of worms → about 1/4 lb of scraps per day

Two caveats. First, a brand-new bin eats less — the worms are stressed from shipping and settling in, so feed lightly for the first two to four weeks. Second, these are averages across a whole population; tiny baby worms eat less, big adults eat more. Don’t weigh your banana peels with a jeweler’s scale. Rough is fine.

Worked examples

Household Scraps per day (rough) Starter worms Notes
1 person, light cooking ~1/4 lb 1/2 lb (~500 worms) Start small; the population will grow
2 people, regular cooking ~1/2 lb 1 lb (~1,000 worms) The classic starter colony
Family of 3–4 ~1 lb 2 lb (~2,000 worms) Or start with 1 lb and let them multiply

These are starting points, not prescriptions. Actual scraps vary wildly — a household that juices vegetables daily produces far more than one that eats out most nights. When in doubt, start conservative: overfeeding causes nearly every beginner problem, from smelly bins to fruit fly invasions.

Start smaller than you think

Here’s the beginner-friendly secret: red wiggler populations grow quickly under good conditions. A one-pound starter colony fed sensibly becomes a much larger workforce within a few months — for free.

That means starting with one pound when your scraps “need” two isn’t a mistake; it’s a strategy. The worms multiply to meet the food supply. What you must not do is the reverse: dumping a full pound of daily scraps into a brand-new bin on day one. New bins need time. Feed lightly, watch the food disappear, and increase gradually. The bin surface area rule

Worms also need elbow room. A useful rule of thumb: about one square foot of bin surface per pound of worms. A standard 10-gallon storage tote offers roughly two square feet of surface, which comfortably houses one to two pounds of red wigglers.

This is another reason red wigglers suit apartments — they’re surface feeders, so they use horizontal space efficiently. If you’re still choosing a species, see best worms for composting.

Signs you have the right number

You don’t need to count worms. Watch the food:

  • Scraps mostly gone in 2–3 days — you’re in the sweet spot.
  • Food piling up uneaten — feed less, or give the population time to catch up.
  • Bin smells bad — classic overfeeding. Cut back and add dry bedding.
  • Worms look thin and food vanishes instantly — you could feed more, or your population is ready to grow.

The bin tells you what it needs. Listen to it for the first month and you’ll develop a feel faster than any chart can teach.

What if your scraps grow over time?

Households change. Maybe you start cooking more, a roommate moves in, or you discover the joy of juicing. The good news: a worm bin scales up gracefully. Increase feeding gradually — no more than a small handful extra per feeding — and the population will expand to meet the new supply over several weeks.

If your scraps permanently outgrow one bin, the apartment-friendly answer is a second small bin rather than one giant one. Two modest totes are easier to lift, easier to keep balanced, and give you a backup colony if one bin has a bad week. Stack them in the same closet corner and you’re done.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Expecting full capacity in week one. New bins need two to four weeks to settle. Patience now prevents problems later.
  • Confusing count with weight. Worms are sold by the pound (~1,000 red wigglers per pound), not by the worm. A “500-worm” listing and a “half-pound” listing are roughly the same thing.
  • Buying for your dream scraps, not your real scraps. Size for what you actually throw away today.
  • Adding more worms to fix overfeeding. The fix for uneaten food is less food, not more mouths.

FAQ

Can I start with just a handful of worms? Yes — a small starter colony works, it just processes scraps slowly at first. The population will grow to match your feeding over a few months.

Do worms multiply on their own? Yes, as long as they’re fed, moist, and comfortable. You should never need to buy worms twice for the same bin.

How long until my bin reaches full capacity? Typically a few months from a standard starter colony. Feed gradually and let the population build — rushing it causes most beginner headaches.

What if I produce more scraps than my worms can eat? Feed what they can handle and freeze the rest — frozen scraps keep perfectly and can be fed later. Don’t dump the surplus in the bin hoping for the best.
