Red Wigglers vs European Nightcrawlers: Which Wins?

Published October 15, 20265 min read

For most apartment worm bins, red wigglers win. They reproduce faster, stay near the surface where your food scraps are, and tolerate typical indoor temperatures. European nightcrawlers are bigger, dig deeper, and handle a wider temperature range — excellent in deeper bins or cooler spots, and some people happily keep both species together.

Meet the contenders

Red wigglers (Eisenia fetida) are the classic composting worm: small (two to three inches), reddish, and happiest in the top few inches of bedding. They are the species behind most home vermicomposting guides, including our best worms for composting overview.

European nightcrawlers (Eisenia hortensis) are their larger cousins: three to five inches long, paler, and inclined to burrow deeper. They are a genuine composting species — not to be confused with Canadian nightcrawlers (Lumbricus terrestris), the garden earthworms sold as fishing bait.

Both eat roughly half their body weight in food scraps per day, and both produce excellent castings. The differences that matter are about how they live, not how well they compost.

Head-to-head comparison

Trait Red wigglers European nightcrawlers
Size 2–3 inches 3–5 inches
Feeding zone Surface, top few inches Deeper bedding
Reproduction Faster — population builds quickly Slower — takes longer to reach capacity
Temperature tolerance Typical indoor temps (~55–80°F) Wider range, tolerates cooler conditions

Castings quality Excellent Excellent
Availability Widely available, usually cheaper per lb Available, often costs more per lb
Fishing bonus Usable bait Popular bait species

Which reproduces faster?

Red wigglers, by a clear margin. In a well-fed bin their population climbs fast enough that most beginners never need to buy worms twice — the colony grows to match the food supply within a few months. European nightcrawlers multiply too, just more slowly, so a new bin takes longer to hit its stride.

If you want a self-sustaining bin as quickly as possible, that speed matters.

Which handles temperature swings better?

European nightcrawlers tolerate a wider range, including cooler temperatures that would slow red wigglers down. That makes them the better pick for a bin in a cool basement, an unheated mudroom, or a garage that stays above freezing.

In a normal heated apartment, though, both species are comfortable. Red wigglers thrive at typical indoor temperatures, so the European advantage only matters if your bin lives somewhere chilly.

Does bin depth matter?

Yes — this is the most underrated difference. Red wigglers are surface feeders, so they are perfectly happy in a standard shallow tote with eight to twelve inches of bedding. European nightcrawlers like to roam deeper, and they do their best work in bins with twelve inches of bedding or more.

If you’re building a basic DIY worm bin from a storage tote, red wigglers are the natural fit. If you’re running a deep stacked system or a large outdoor bin, Europeans get more room to show what they can do.

What about castings quality?

Honestly? Both produce rich, dark castings that houseplants love. There is no meaningful difference in castings quality between the two species for a home composter. Choose based on your bin and your climate, not on promises about “better” castings — anyone selling you that distinction is selling you something.

Can you mix them? The “power couple” bin

Some experienced vermicomposters run mixed bins with both species — red wigglers working the surface layer and European nightcrawlers working the depths. It’s a documented practitioner approach, not a miracle hack: the idea is simply to use more of the bin’s volume.

If you try it, give them a deep bin (twelve inches plus) so the nightcrawlers have somewhere to go, and feed across the surface as usual. Keep expectations modest — a mixed bin won’t compost twice as fast. It just puts two feeding zones to work. Start with red wigglers alone if you’re new; you can always add Europeans later once the bin is established.

Which should YOU choose?

  • Standard apartment tote bin → red wigglers. They are cheaper, multiply faster, and are built for shallow bins.
  • Deep bin or cool spot → European nightcrawlers. They use the depth and shrug off cooler temperatures.
  • Can’t decide → start with red wigglers. You can add Europeans later; the two species coexist fine.

Either way, size your starter colony to your kitchen scraps — our worm calculator does the math for you, or read how many worms you need.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Buying bait-shop “nightcrawlers” by mistake. Those are usually Canadian nightcrawlers — garden earthworms, not composters. Check for the name Eisenia hortensis.
  • Putting Europeans in a very shallow bin. They’ll survive, but you won’t see their best work.
  • Overthinking the choice. Both species compost well. Picking either one and feeding it sensibly beats months of deliberation.

FAQ

Will red wigglers and European nightcrawlers interbreed? No. They are different species and won’t crossbreed. A mixed bin just holds two separate populations sharing the same bedding.

Can I switch species later? Yes. Add the new species to an established bin and the populations will sort themselves out. There’s no need to start over.

Which is better if I also want fishing bait? European nightcrawlers are the more popular bait species because of their size, though red wigglers catch fish too.

Do I need to pick just one species? No. Mixed bins are a legitimate, documented approach. But beginners should master one species first — fewer variables while you’re learning.
