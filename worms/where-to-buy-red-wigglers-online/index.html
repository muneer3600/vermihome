The best place to buy red wigglers online for most beginners is Brothers Worm Farm for a small starter colony, or Meme’s Worms if you want a full pound of pure red wigglers with free shipping. Both sell the right species, both ship in the US, and both state their prices clearly. The supplier you should avoid is the one that cannot tell you the species — and the listing that sells worms by count instead of by weight.

We have not ordered from these suppliers for this guide. Prices and policies below come from the suppliers’ own product pages, checked 6 November 2026. Where a price could not be verified, we say so instead of guessing.

1. Brothers Worm Farm — best for a small starter colony

Brothers Worm Farm is a specialist composting-worm farm that ships throughout the 48 contiguous US states. It states that its population is Eisenia fetida — pure red wigglers — and that it works to keep the population as close to pure as possible. That purity statement matters, because mixed-species batches can include Indian blues (Perionyx excavatus), which behave differently in a bin.

Prices verified 6 November 2026 on the supplier’s own product page: 100 red wigglers for $26, 1/4 lb (about 250 worms) for $30, 1/2 lb (about 500 worms) for $38, and 2 lb (about 2,000 worms) for $92. Free shipping is stated on the product page. Note that several sizes were listed as out of stock on the day we checked, including the 100-worm and 1/4 lb options — check availability before you plan around a size.

The 1 lb price was not directly listed on the page we checked. Do not assume it from the other sizes: small quantities carry a premium per pound, so 1/2 lb at $38 does not mean 1 lb costs $76, and 2 lb at $92 does not mean 1 lb costs $46. The honest reading is that Brothers is cheapest per pound when you buy 2 lb, and most expensive per pound when you buy 100 worms. For a beginner, the 1/2 lb pack at $38 is the sweet spot: enough worms to start a tray-stack bin, cheap enough that a mistake does not sting.

Brothers recommends 1 lb of worms per square foot of bin surface, and 1 lb for a first tray of a stackable bin. That matches VermiHome’s own guidance. It does not ship live worms to Hawaii, Alaska, or Puerto Rico.

Best for: first-time buyers who want 1/2 lb of pure red wigglers with free shipping.

2. Meme’s Worms — best for a full pound of pure red wigglers

Meme’s Worms is a family farm in Valdosta, Georgia, selling composting worms, castings, and coco coir. Its pure red wiggler listing states the species as Eisenia fetida, sells by weight, and gives a count of about 800–1,200 worms per pound for reference only.

Price verified 6 November 2026: 1 lb of pure red wigglers for $64.99, with free shipping, reduced from $79.99. The same price applies to its pre-order listing, which ships on the next scheduled date — the listing we checked showed worms shipping 16 November. The 1 lb listing was sold out on the day we checked, with the pre-order available instead. If you need worms this week rather than next, check the pre-order ship date before you pay — pre-orders are non-refundable.

Meme’s ships composting products every Monday and Tuesday by UPS Ground or USPS Priority Mail, and orders typically arrive within 2–3 business days. Shipping may shift around extreme heat or cold to protect the worms. That Monday–Tuesday rhythm is worth knowing: order on a Wednesday and your worms may not ship until the following Monday.

Meme’s also sells the Worm Bucket kit ($64.99) and worm-and-chow combos, which can be good value if you need bedding and food as well as worms. A 1 lb worms-plus-chow-plus-comb combo was listed at $49.14 on a reseller page, but that price was not verified on Meme’s own site — treat it as a lead to check, not a price to rely on.

Best for: buyers who want a full pound of pure red wigglers and can wait for the next Monday–Tuesday ship window.

3. Worms4Earth — cheapest headline range, verify the size

Worms4Earth is a Pensacola, Florida supplier selling live composting worms and vermiculture supplies, including the Worm Factory 360. Its homepage lists red wigglers at $49.95–$79.90, and it states a guaranteed live delivery policy: if worms are harmed in transit, the supplier says it will replace the order quickly and without hassle.

We could not verify an exact per-size price for Worms4Earth on 6 November 2026. The $49.95–$79.90 range is the range shown on the homepage, and it does not say which size costs $49.95 and which costs $79.90. It is probably the range from a small pack to 1 lb, but that is a guess — do not quote it as a 1 lb price. Before you order, open the red wiggler product page and read the size and price together. If 1 lb is at the low end of that range, Worms4Earth is the cheapest full pound in this guide. If 1 lb is at the high end, it is not.

Worms4Earth also lists a composter’s combo — 1,000 red wigglers plus 500 European nightcrawlers — at $109.85, reduced from $124.85. A mixed-species starter can work well, with red wigglers on the surface and nightcrawlers deeper, but only buy it if you understand the mix. See red wigglers vs European nightcrawlers first.

Best for: buyers willing to check the product page carefully — the headline range looks cheap, but the per-size price is unverified.

4. Uncle Jim’s Worm Farm — biggest name, watch the species mix

Uncle Jim’s Worm Farm has been raising worms since the 1970s — its site says 1973 in one place and 1976 in another, so treat the exact year as unverified, but the business is genuinely long-established. It sells worms, bins, and castings, and it is the supplier most likely to appear in Amazon search results as well as on its own site.

Prices verified 6 November 2026: a 2,000-worm red composting mix for $94.95 plus $10.50 shipping, shipped by FedEx 2-Day Express, with a stated 100% guaranteed live delivery and free reshipment if there are transit problems. A 250-worm red composting mix was listed at $32.95, reduced from $39.95, with $8.99 shipping by USPS Priority or 2-Day FedEx.

The important detail is the word “mix”. Uncle Jim’s red composting mix may contain several compost worm species, including Eisenia fetida, Eisenia hortensis, and Perionyx excavatus. That is stated on the product page. A mix is not a defect — mixed bins can work well — but it is not the same as the pure Eisenia fetida that Brothers and Meme’s sell. If you want pure red wigglers and nothing else, buy from a supplier that states purity. If you do not mind a mix, Uncle Jim’s 2,000-worm pack at $94.95 plus shipping is about $47.48 per pound equivalent if you treat 2,000 worms as roughly 2 lb — the cheapest per-pound rate for a large order in this guide, with the mix caveat attached.

Uncle Jim’s ships Monday through Friday, and says 99% of orders arrive without a problem. It ships only in the contiguous US, not to Hawaii, Alaska, or overseas.

Best for: large orders and buyers who do not mind a mixed-species colony. Not the best choice if you specifically want pure Eisenia fetida.

5. Amazon — Nature’s Little Recyclers — convenient, expensive per worm

Amazon is where many beginners start, because it is familiar and fast. Nature’s Little Recyclers sells red wigglers on Amazon in a 250+ worm pack. A review of that pack describes it as a long-term compost companion and relatively good value for the quantity, while noting that the price per worm can be higher than bulk purchases elsewhere.

We could not verify a current price for Nature’s Little Recyclers on 6 November 2026. Amazon search results list the product, but no stable price was shown in the pages we checked. Do not order on the basis of a price in an old review.

The structural problem with Amazon worm listings is not the seller but the format. Amazon sells worms by count — 100, 250, 1,000 — while specialist suppliers sell by weight. A 250-worm pack is about 1/4 lb. If it costs $30, you are paying $120 per pound. That is why a small Amazon pack can look cheap and still be the most expensive worms in this guide. If you buy on Amazon, convert the count to weight first: roughly 1,000 worms to the pound, so divide the price by the count and multiply by 1,000 to get the price per pound. And check the species name on the listing. If it does not say Eisenia fetida, do not buy it for composting — pet-store “red wigglers” are sometimes a different species entirely.

Amazon’s advantage is speed and buyer protection. If you need worms this week and a specialist supplier is sold out or ships only on Mondays, Amazon can be the practical choice. Just do the per-pound maths before you click buy.

Best for: urgent orders and buyers who value Amazon’s delivery and returns. Check the price and the species on the listing — neither was verified here.

What to order for your first bin

Order 1/2 lb to 1 lb of pure red wigglers. That is roughly 500–1,000 worms, and it is the standard starter colony for a household bin. Our worm calculator will size it to your household, and how many worms you need explains the maths.

FAQ

How much do red wigglers cost? Expect $38 for 1/2 lb and $50–$65 for 1 lb from a specialist supplier, plus shipping if it is not included. Prices verified 6 November 2026: Brothers Worm Farm 1/2 lb $38, Meme’s Worms 1 lb $64.99 with free shipping, Worms4Earth $49.95–$79.90 range (exact size unverified). Prices change often — check the supplier’s page before you order.

Do suppliers guarantee live delivery? The specialist suppliers in this guide all state a live-delivery guarantee, usually with free reshipment. Read the terms: most require you to open the box promptly and report a problem the same day.
