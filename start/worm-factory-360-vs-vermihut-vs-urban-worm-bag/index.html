Worm Factory 360 vs VermiHut Plus vs Urban Worm Bag: Which Is Best for You?

If you want the short answer: buy the VermiHut Plus if you want the cheapest good bin, buy the Worm Factory 360 if you want the most-reviewed bin with a five-year warranty, and buy the Urban Worm Bag if you hate harvesting and are willing to pay for the easiest method. All three are good bins. The wrong choice is not a bad bin — it is a good bin bought for the wrong household.

These are the three most-cited worm bins in US roundups, and they represent the two systems that matter: tray stacks (Worm Factory 360 and VermiHut Plus) and flow-through (Urban Worm Bag). This guide compares them head-to-head on price, harvest, capacity, smell, and who each one actually suits.

Prices below were checked on 6 November 2026. Prices change often, so check the seller’s page before you order. We have not hands-on tested these bins. This comparison is based on published specifications, seller listings, and published reviews.

Price: the VermiHut wins by about $50

The VermiHut Plus is the cheapest of the three by a clear margin. Farmstand’s October 2026 roundup listed it at $82, and Amazon has listed it at $69 against a $79 list price — so $69–$82 is the honest range. The Worm Factory 360 was listed at $134.95 by EarthEasy and $129.95 by Worms4Earth on 6 November 2026. The Urban Worm Bag V2 was $139 at EarthEasy and $139.99 at Uncle Jim’s Worm Farm.

For the Worm Factory 360, the extra $50 over the VermiHut buys US manufacturing, a five-year warranty, and the longest track record of any bin in this guide. For the Urban Worm Bag, the extra $70 over the VermiHut buys a different harvest method entirely — not a better version of the same thing, but a different way of getting compost out. Those are the only two reasons to pay more. If neither reason matters to you, buy the VermiHut and spend the saving on worms.

Remember that none of these prices includes worms. A starter colony costs $38 for 1/2 lb from Brothers Worm Farm or $64.99 for 1 lb from Meme’s Worms, both checked 6 November 2026. See where to buy red wigglers online for the full supplier comparison.

How each system works

Tray stacks: Worm Factory 360 and VermiHut Plus

Both tray stacks work the same way. You start with bedding and worms in one tray. When the food in that tray is mostly processed, you put a fresh tray with bedding and food on top. The worms migrate upward through the mesh floor into the new tray. After a few weeks, the bottom tray is mostly finished castings, and you lift it off and empty it.

The difference between the two is in the box, not the idea. The Worm Factory 360 ships with four trays. The VermiHut Plus ships with five. That extra tray matters more than it sounds: it gives you more buffer before you need to buy extra trays, and it forgives the most common beginner mistake, which is adding food to a tray that is already full. A full tray with nowhere for worms to go gets wet and airless, and that is when a bin starts to smell. See why your worm bin smells for what that looks like and how to fix it.

The Worm Factory 360 also comes with a five-year warranty, and it is made in the US. The VermiHut Plus has no warranty figure we could verify — check the listing before you buy if a warranty matters to you.

One warning for the Worm Factory 360: the current version no longer includes the worm ladder, moisture meter, thermometer, or sprinkler tray that older setup guides show. EarthEasy notes this on the product page. If a video shows those accessories, it is showing an older bundle, and you will not get them in the box.

Flow-through: Urban Worm Bag V2

The Urban Worm Bag is a tall fabric bag. You feed from the top and harvest from the bottom. As material moves down through the bag, it finishes. When the bottom is ready, you open a flap and scrape castings out with a small tool. The worms stay near the top, where the fresh food is, so you barely have to sort them out.

That is the easiest harvest of any bin you can buy. It is also the slowest to get going. A flow-through bag needs a deep column of material before the bottom is finished enough to harvest, and a new bag with a small starter colony will take months to build that column. A tray stack, by contrast, can give you a first finished tray in roughly two to three months, because you are harvesting one small tray at a time rather than waiting for a whole column.

The bag is breathable fabric, which helps airflow. Good airflow is the main defence against smell in any bin, and fabric does it naturally. The bag also folds flat for storage and moving, which no tray stack does — useful if you rent and may move.

Capacity: who each bin suits

Capacity is where buyers most often get this wrong, because every manufacturer states capacity at full maturity, not at setup. A new bin with 1 lb of worms processes about 1/2 lb of food a day, whatever the bin’s stated maximum is. The stated maximum is what the bin can handle once the colony has grown for several months.

  • One person, light cooking: any of the three is more bin than you need at first. Buy the cheapest — the VermiHut Plus — and start with 1/2 lb of worms. You will not fill five trays for months.
  • Two people, regular cooking: the VermiHut Plus or the Worm Factory 360, both with 1 lb of worms. The VermiHut’s fifth tray is a small advantage here.
  • Family of three or four, daily cooking: the VermiHut Plus at full capacity (the manufacturer states up to 10 lb of worms and up to 5 lb of scraps a day) or the Urban Worm Bag, which has no tray limit to outgrow. The Worm Factory 360 can do this too, but you will likely need to buy extra trays.
  • Big household, or you want to compost for the garden as well as the kitchen: the Urban Worm Bag. A flow-through system scales by height, not by buying trays, and its harvest stays easy as the colony grows.

If you are unsure how much your household produces, do the maths first with how many worms you need and the worm calculator. Buying too big is the most expensive beginner mistake: an oversized bin with too few worms goes sour, and the fix is not a better bin but less food and more patience.

Harvest: the real difference

Harvest is where these three bins genuinely differ, and it is the decision most buyers underweight. You will harvest many times over the life of a bin. A harvest method you dislike is a bin you will stop using.

Tray stacks (both): lift off the bottom tray, empty it, put it back on top. Simple, and you can see what you are doing. The work is lifting a tray that weighs several kilos when wet, and checking that most worms have migrated up before you empty it. If worms are still in the tray, you wait a week or pick them out. Most people find this acceptable. It takes about 15 minutes.

Flow-through bag: open the bottom flap and scrape. No lifting, no waiting for migration, almost no worm sorting. It takes about 10 minutes and is less messy. The cost is the wait at the start: months before the first proper harvest, and a learning curve in judging when the bottom is actually finished. Harvest too early and you scrape out unfinished material with worms in it.

If you know you will hate sorting worms, buy the bag. If you want castings sooner and do not mind lifting a tray, buy a stack. There is no wrong answer here, only a mismatch between the method and your patience.

Which should you buy?

Buy the VermiHut Plus 5-Tray if: you want the cheapest full-size bin, you are buying your first bin, or your household is one to four people. It is the best value in this comparison, and the fifth tray is genuinely useful. Price: $69–$82.

Buy the Worm Factory 360 if: you want the most-reviewed bin, US manufacturing, and a five-year warranty, and you are happy to pay about $50 more for that. It is the safest choice, not the best-value choice. Price: $129.95–$134.95.

Buy the Urban Worm Bag V2 if: you hate the idea of sorting worms, you have a larger household, and you are patient enough to wait months for the first harvest. It is the easiest bin to live with long-term and the most expensive to buy. Price: $139–$139.99.

Buy none of these yet if: your budget is under $65. Build a DIY worm bin for under $40 instead, or buy the Worm Bucket for $64.99 if you need the smallest possible footprint. A tote composts just as well — you give up the easy harvest, not the compost.

Whichever you choose, read how to set up a store-bought worm bin before it arrives. The first 30 days decide whether the bin becomes a habit or a cupboard ornament.

FAQ

Is the Worm Factory 360 worth $50 more than the VermiHut Plus? Only if you value the five-year warranty, US manufacturing, and the longest review history. The composting is the same. If budget matters, the VermiHut Plus is the better buy.

Does the Urban Worm Bag really make harvesting easier? Yes — scraping castings from a bottom flap is easier than lifting and sorting trays. The catch is the wait: a new bag takes months to build a harvestable column, while a tray stack can give you a first tray in two to three months.

Which bin is best for a family of four? The VermiHut Plus at full capacity or the Urban Worm Bag. Both handle a larger colony without buying extra trays. Start with 1–2 lb of worms and let the colony grow — do not buy a big colony on day one.
