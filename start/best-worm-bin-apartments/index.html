Best Worm Bin for Apartments: 7 Picks for Small Spaces (2026)

The best worm bin for most apartments is the VermiHut Plus 5-Tray. It costs less than the Worm Factory 360, stacks vertically so it fits in a closet or under a counter, and its five trays give beginners room to make mistakes without overloading the bin. If you want the easiest harvest and have the budget, the Urban Worm Bag V2 is the better pick — but it costs more.

There is no single best bin, because apartments differ. A one-person household with a kitchen corner needs something different from a family of four with a balcony closet. This guide compares seven options — six you can buy and one you can build — by the things that actually matter in a small space: price, footprint, smell control, and how hard it is to get the finished compost out.

Prices below were checked on 6 November 2026 from seller and retailer listings. Prices change often, and several of these bins go on sale, so check the seller’s page before you order. We have not hands-on tested these bins. This comparison is based on published specifications, seller listings, and published reviews, plus VermiHome’s own troubleshooting knowledge about what goes wrong in apartment bins.

1. VermiHut Plus 5-Tray — best overall for apartments

The VermiHut Plus is a five-tray stacking system. You start with one working tray, and as the worms finish the food in it, you add a tray on top with fresh bedding and food. The worms migrate upward, and the bottom tray becomes finished castings you can lift off and empty. Tray stacks suit apartments because the bin grows upward, not outward.

The price is its biggest advantage. Farmstand’s October 2026 roundup listed it at $82, and Amazon has listed it at $69 with a $79 list price — so expect to pay roughly $69–$82 depending on the seller and the day. That makes it the cheapest full-size tray system in this guide, and the only one under $100 at its usual price.

Its stated capacity is 50 litres, and the manufacturer says a five-tray system can support up to 10 lb of worms and process up to 5 lb of food scraps a day at full capacity. Take the top end of that with a pinch of salt — a new bin with 1 lb of worms will not process 5 lb a day. What the five trays really give a beginner is buffer room: you are less likely to overload one tray and create the wet, airless conditions that cause smell. If your bin does start to smell, the fix is usually the same whatever bin you own — see why your worm bin smells.

The VermiHut Plus includes accessories to get you started, and its ventilation is repeatedly mentioned in roundups as forgiving for beginners. Best for: first-time buyers, one to four people, anyone who wants a full-size bin for under $100.

2. Worm Factory 360 — best established tray system

The Worm Factory 360 is the best-known tray-stack bin in the US. It ships with four trays, and you can add more. EarthEasy listed it at $134.95 on 6 November 2026, and Worms4Earth listed it at $129.95, so budget around $130–$135 for the bin alone.

It is made in the US and comes with a five-year warranty. One thing to know before you buy: the current version no longer includes the worm ladder, moisture meter, thermometer, or sprinkler tray that older instructions mention. If a setup video shows those accessories, it is showing an older bundle.

Compared with the VermiHut Plus, you are paying roughly $50 more for a similar tray-stack idea with one fewer tray in the box. What you get for that is a longer track record, US manufacturing, and the warranty. For many buyers that is worth it. For a tight budget, it is not.

Best for: buyers who want the most-reviewed bin and a five-year warranty, and do not mind paying for it.

3. Urban Worm Bag V2 — easiest harvest

The Urban Worm Bag V2 works differently from every other bin in this guide. It is a tall fabric bag, not a stack of trays. You feed from the top, and when the bottom is finished, you open a flap at the base and scrape out castings — no lifting trays, no sorting worms by hand. That is called a flow-through system, and it is the easiest harvest method you can buy.

It costs $139 at EarthEasy and $139.99 at Uncle Jim’s Worm Farm, checked 6 November 2026. That makes it the most expensive bin in this guide, and the price does not include worms or bedding.

The bag is breathable fabric, which helps airflow and smell control, and it takes up a small amount of floor space because it is tall rather than wide. The downside is patience: a flow-through bag needs time to build up a deep column of finished material before the bottom harvest works well. If you want castings in the first month, a tray stack will feel faster. If you are happy to wait and want the least fiddly harvest for years afterward, this is the bin to buy. Our head-to-head comparison covers the trade-off in detail: Worm Factory 360 vs VermiHut Plus vs Urban Worm Bag.

Best for: patient buyers, larger households, anyone who hates sorting worms out of castings.

4. VIVOSUN 5-Layer — best budget tray stack after the VermiHut

The VIVOSUN 5-Layer is a five-layer stacking bin with a 50-litre capacity, sold as a kit with the accessories needed to start. A garden-comparison site listed it at $79.99 in 2026, which puts it just under the VermiHut Plus’s usual price and well under the Worm Factory 360.

It follows the same idea as the other tray stacks: worms migrate upward into fresh trays, and you lift off a finished layer to harvest. The kit format is genuinely useful for a first bin — you are not hunting for separate parts. The brand is better known for grow equipment than for vermicomposting, so there is less long-term worm-specific support and fewer published long-term reviews than for the Worm Factory 360. That matters less than it sounds: a tray stack is a simple device, and the care is the same whatever brand you buy. See how to set up an indoor worm bin for the setup that applies to all of them.

Best for: buyers who want five trays for under $80 and do not need a worm-specific brand name.

5. Blutezeit 5-Tray — large capacity, verify the price

The Blutezeit 5-Tray (sold as Blütezeit on Amazon) is a five-tray stack with a 53-quart capacity — about 50 litres, the same class as the VIVOSUN and VermiHut. It is sold as an all-in-one starter kit, and roundups list it as a good pick for larger capacity and beginners.

We could not verify a current price for this bin on 6 November 2026. The Amazon product page we checked showed no price in its text, and roundups do not quote a stable figure for it. Do not trust a price quoted from an old roundup — check the listing on the day you buy. What we can say is that it belongs in the same budget tray-stack group as the VIVOSUN: five trays, similar capacity, similar idea. If it is priced near $70–$80 when you check, it is a fair alternative to the VIVOSUN. If it is priced near the Worm Factory 360, buy the Worm Factory instead — you get the warranty and the track record for the same money.

Best for: buyers who find it on sale at a budget price. Not a first choice at an unknown price.

6. Worm Bucket — smallest footprint, smallest capacity

The Worm Bucket, sold by Meme’s Worms, is a single-bucket indoor composter kit. It costs $64.99, reduced from $69.99, checked 6 November 2026 on the seller’s own site. Its selling point is size: it takes up about one square foot of floor space, so it fits on a kitchen counter, in a cupboard, or beside a bin where a tray stack will not fit.

The trade-off is capacity. A single bucket holds a small colony and processes a small amount of food. It is a good fit for one person who cooks at home a few times a week, and a poor fit for a family of four. Harvesting is also more hands-on than a tray stack: there is no finished tray to lift off, so you harvest by hand from a single chamber.

Buy this one only if space is your hardest constraint. If you have room for a tray stack, a tray stack will process more food and be easier to harvest.

Best for: single-person households, tiny kitchens, renters who need the smallest possible bin.

7. DIY tote — cheapest, and the control group

You can build a perfectly good worm bin from a plastic tote for under $40, usually well under it — a $10 tote, some bedding, and a drill. Our guide covers the whole build: DIY worm bin.

The DIY tote is the control group for this whole roundup. Every bought bin has to beat it on something real: easier harvest, better smell control, or a smaller footprint. A tray stack beats it on harvest. A flow-through bag beats it on harvest too. A bought bin does not beat it on price — nothing here does — and it does not beat it on simplicity, because a tote has no parts to break.

FAQ

What is the best worm bin for a small apartment? For most apartments, the VermiHut Plus 5-Tray. It is the cheapest full-size tray system at $69–$82, it stacks vertically, and its five trays forgive beginner overfeeding. If harvest ease matters most and your budget reaches $139, the Urban Worm Bag V2 is better.

Can I just use a plastic tote instead? Yes. A DIY tote under $40 composts just as well as a bought bin. You give up the easy harvest and the tidy look, and you save $25–$100. See DIY worm bin.
