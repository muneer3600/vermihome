The best apartment composting starter kit is the Worm Factory 360 4-Tray with Worms at $171.95. It is the only kit in this guide that includes the bin, half a pound of red wiggler worms, bedding, and accessories in a single box — you open it, set it up, and you are composting the same day. If your budget is tighter, the VermiHut Plus 5-Tray at $69–$82 is the best-value worm kit, and the EarthEasy Bokashi Dual-Bucket kit at $42.70 is the cheapest all-in-one kit of any kind.

A starter kit is different from a bin. A bin is just a container; a kit is a container plus the things you need to start — bedding, worms or bokashi bran, and instructions. That matters for a first-timer, because the most common beginner failure is not the bin, it is the missing piece: a bin with no worms, a bokashi bucket with no bran, or bedding you forgot to buy. These five kits are judged on completeness first and price second.

1. Worm Factory 360 4-Tray with Worms — best all-in-one

The Worm Factory 360 with worms costs $171.95 from the maker’s own store, Nature’s Footprint. What is included: four stacking trays, a base with a spigot for worm tea, start-up bedding, an accessory kit with a hand rake and scraper, and half a pound (about 500) red wiggler worms, shipped separately on a redemption voucher so they arrive alive. The bin without worms lists at about $134.95–$139.99, so the worm bundle adds roughly $32–$37 — cheaper than buying worms separately at $40–$55.

This is the most foolproof start in vermicomposting. There is nothing else to buy: no separate worm order, no bedding run, no guessing which tray goes where. The four trays expand to more as your colony grows, and the spigot lets you drain worm tea without opening the bin. The only real drawback is the price — $171.95 is the most expensive kit in this guide — and the worms ship separately, so they arrive up to two weeks after the bin. Order it, set up the bin with the included bedding, and add the worms when they arrive.

Best for: first-timers who want one box, one order, and zero missing pieces.

2. VermiHut Plus 5-Tray — best-value worm kit

The VermiHut Plus costs $69.00 on Amazon and $82.00 in a November 2026 roundup, making it the cheapest proper stacking worm bin you can buy. It has five trays, enhanced airflow and odour control, and accessories included. What it does not include is worms, so add $40–$55 for half a pound of red wigglers (see where to buy red wigglers online) and $0–$15 for bedding if you do not use free cardboard.

All-in, the VermiHut route costs roughly $115–$150 — still $20–$55 less than the Worm Factory bundle — and you get five trays instead of four. The trade-off is the extra orders: you must buy worms and bedding separately, and you must time them to arrive together. If you are comfortable placing two orders instead of one, this is the best value in worm composting. If you want a single box, pay the extra for the Worm Factory bundle above.

Best for: buyers who want a proper stacking bin at the lowest all-in price and do not mind ordering worms separately.

3. Urban Worm Bag V2 — best for easiest harvesting

The Urban Worm Bag V2 costs $139.00 from EarthEasy and $139.99 from Uncle Jim’s Worm Farm. It is a continuous-flow fabric bin on a metal frame: you add food and bedding at the top, and you harvest finished castings from the bottom through a zippered opening, without disturbing the worms at all. It holds up to 5.3 cubic feet of vermicompost, far more than any stacking tray bin.

Worms are not included, so add $40–$55, and bedding is not included either, though a single 1.4 lb coir brick at $14.99 is enough to start. All-in, expect roughly $185–$210. The bag’s advantage is harvesting: there is no tray lifting, no hand-sorting, and no worm migration waiting period. Its disadvantage for apartments is size — it is taller and wider than a stacking bin, and it needs floor space rather than a shelf. If you have a spare corner and hate harvesting, this is the kit to buy.

Best for: anyone who wants harvesting to be a five-minute job, and who has floor space to spare.

4. EarthEasy Bokashi Dual-Bucket Starter Kit — cheapest kit

Not everyone wants worms, and bokashi is the no-worm alternative: you ferment food scraps — including meat, dairy, and cooked food that worms cannot eat — in a sealed bucket with bokashi bran, then bury the result in soil or add it to a compost pile. The EarthEasy Bokashi Food Waste Starter Kit costs $42.70 and includes two bokashi buckets, two lids, and 5 lb of premium bokashi bran. That is a complete system for $42.70, with nothing else to buy.

Bokashi has real limits for apartments: the fermented scraps still need somewhere to go — a garden bed, a planter, or a compost drop-off — and a bucket of fermenting food is not the same as a worm bin that produces castings you can use directly on houseplants. But if you have a balcony garden or access to soil, bokashi handles the widest range of food waste of any system in this guide, and it is the cheapest way to start composting in an apartment. A single-bucket version, the Essential Bokashi Compost Bin at $42.00, is also available if you want to start with one bucket.

Best for: the cheapest possible start, and households that eat meat and dairy and want to compost all of it.

5. SCD Probiotics All Seasons Indoor Composter Kit — best bokashi starter

The SCD Probiotics All Seasons Indoor Composter Kit costs $50.00, down from a list price of $64.95, from the maker’s own store. It includes a 4.4-gallon Organko composter bin with a presser, strainer, scoop, and compost tea cup, plus 1 gallon (2 lb) of All Seasons Bokashi bran. Refill bran costs $17.33 for a 1-gallon bag, $38.50 for 2 gallons, or $49.95 for 3 gallons.

At $50, it costs $7.30 more than the EarthEasy dual-bucket kit, and it includes only one bucket instead of two — so why pick it? Because it is the original, best-documented bokashi kit, with a spigot for draining bokashi tea and a full accessory set, and because a single bucket is the right size for a one- or two-person apartment. Two buckets are better for a family; one bucket is enough for a single person or a couple. If you want the most proven bokashi kit rather than the cheapest, this is it.

Best for: single people and couples who want a proven single-bucket bokashi kit with a tea spigot.

Worm kit or bokashi kit — which is right for you?

Choose a worm kit if you want finished compost you can use directly on houseplants, you like the idea of a living system, and you eat mostly plant-based food. Worm castings are the best houseplant fertiliser in this guide’s scope (see best worm castings for houseplants).

Choose a bokashi kit if you eat meat and dairy, you do not want live animals in your kitchen, or you have somewhere to bury fermented scraps. Bokashi is simpler, cheaper, and handles more types of waste — but it does not produce castings.

You can also run both: a worm bin for vegetable scraps and a bokashi bucket for meat, dairy, and cooked food. That combination composts nearly everything a household produces, and it is the setup many serious apartment composters end up with.

What to check before you order

  • Does the kit include worms or bran? Only the Worm Factory bundle includes worms, and only the bokashi kits include bran. Every other kit needs a second purchase.
  • Where will it live? Measure your space. A stacking bin needs a shelf or corner about 16 inches square; the Urban Worm Bag needs floor space; a bokashi bucket fits under a sink.
  • Where will the output go? Worm castings go on houseplants. Fermented bokashi scraps need soil. If you have neither plants nor soil access, start with worms and houseplants, not bokashi.
  • Shipping. Worms ship separately and cannot go to Hawaii. Bokashi kits ship as normal parcels. Check delivery times before you order, especially for live worms.

FAQ

What is the cheapest way to start composting in an apartment? The EarthEasy Bokashi Dual-Bucket kit at $42.70, all-in. The cheapest worm route is a VermiHut Plus at $69–$82 plus worms at $40–$55, or about $115–$150 all-in.

Do any kits include everything, including worms? Yes — one: the Worm Factory 360 with Worms at $171.95. Every other worm kit needs a separate worm order.

Can I start with a bokashi kit and add worms later? Yes. Bokashi and worms complement each other: bokashi handles meat and dairy, worms handle vegetables and produce castings. Many apartment composters run both.

How long does a starter kit last? A stacking bin or worm bag lasts five years or more. Bokashi buckets last for years, and you only need to buy refill bran — about $17.33 per gallon — as you use it up.
