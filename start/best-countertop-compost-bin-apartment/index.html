A countertop bin does not compost anything. It is a small, lidded bin that sits next to your cutting board and holds scraps for a few days until you empty it into a worm bin, a bokashi bucket, an outdoor bin, or a compost drop-off. That sounds trivial, and it is the reason most apartment composting fails without one: if scraps have nowhere to go while you cook, they go in the trash. This guide compares six bins by the things that matter on a counter: size, smell control, ease of cleaning, and price.

1. Epica Countertop 1.3 gal — best overall

The Epica is the best-selling countertop compost bin on Amazon for a reason: it does the simple job well. Stainless steel does not stain or hold smells the way plastic can, the lid seals tightly, and the replaceable charcoal filter traps odours for about six months before it needs replacing. At 7.5 inches in diameter and 11 inches tall, it fits beside the sink without crowding the counter.

Its 1.3-gallon capacity holds two to three days of scraps for a typical household — the right rhythm, because scraps left longer than three days start to smell whatever bin they are in. Compost Magazine’s 2026 roundup rated it best overall at $29.98, and a November 2026 listing roundup recorded 21,045 ratings averaging 4.6 out of 5.

Best for: most apartments. If you buy one countertop bin, buy this one.

2. OXO Good Grips Easy-Clean 1.75 gal — easiest to use while cooking

The OXO Good Grips bin takes a different approach: a flip-top lid you can open one-handed while holding a peeler, a smooth interior that wipes clean, and a removable bottom for emptying. At 1.75 gallons it is the largest bin in this guide, which suits households that cook a lot — but it also means scraps sit longer, so empty it every two days, not every four.

It costs about $29–$31, roughly the same as the Epica, and Compost Magazine rated it best value at $25.79 in its 2026 roundup — prices vary by seller and by day, so check the listing. The plastic body is lighter than steel and will not dent, but it can stain over time with coffee grounds and tomato scraps. Rinse it when you empty it and it stays clean.

Best for: people who add scraps many times a day while cooking and want one-handed access.

3. OXO Good Grips 0.75 gal — best for tiny counters

The smaller OXO, at 0.75 gallons, is about the size of a large coffee mug. It fits in the gap between a toaster and a coffee maker, and it forces you to empty it every day or two — which is actually a feature, not a flaw, because fresh scraps never reach the smelly stage. Home Compost Guide calls it the best small bin for apartment dwellers and minimalists.

We could not verify a separate current price for the 0.75-gallon size on 6 November 2026 — listings for the OXO line quote $29–$31 for the 1.75-gallon size, and the smaller bin is usually a few dollars less. Check the listing for your size before you order.

Best for: studio apartments, single-person households, and crowded counters.

4. Joseph Joseph Intelligent Waste 1 gal — best with compostable bags

The Joseph Joseph compost caddy holds 1 gallon (4 litres) and is designed around compostable bags: a liner-retaining hole holds the bag in place, and an adjustable air vent reduces moisture buildup without a filter. At $27.50 it is the cheapest named bin in this guide, and its narrow 6.7-inch width fits where wider bins do not.

The vent system is clever but it is not a charcoal filter — in a warm kitchen, a vented bin can smell sooner than a filtered one. Empty it every two days and it is fine. If you like using compostable bags and hate rinsing a bin, this is the best-designed option.

Best for: bag users, narrow counters, and buyers who want the lowest price from a known brand.

5. Utopia Kitchen 1.3 gal — best budget steel bin

The Utopia Kitchen 1.3-gallon stainless steel bin does almost exactly what the Epica does — same capacity, same steel body, same charcoal filter — for $26.99, a few dollars less. Greenwashing Index’s 2026 roundup called it the best balance of performance, durability, and value at that price.

The trade-off is brand and support: Utopia Kitchen is a budget housewares brand, not a composting specialist, and replacement filters are sold by third parties. The bin itself is simple enough that this matters little — a steel bin with a lid and a filter has almost nothing to break.

Best for: buyers who want the Epica’s design at the lowest price.

6. simplehuman Compost Caddy 4 L — best premium feel, price unverified

The simplehuman Compost Caddy is a 4-litre (1.06-gallon) brushed stainless steel bin designed to look like a permanent kitchen fixture rather than a utility bucket. It is the most expensive-feeling bin in this guide, and Home Compost Guide lists it as the premium pick for buyers who want a bin that looks good on the counter.

We could not verify a current price for it on 6 November 2026, so we cannot say whether it is worth the premium. If it is priced near $30–$40, it is a fair alternative to the Epica. If it is priced much higher, the Epica does the same job for less.

Best for: buyers who care about how the bin looks and find it at a fair price.

How to use a countertop bin (so it does not smell)

A countertop bin fails for one reason: scraps sit in it too long. Follow three rules and it will not smell.

  1. Empty it every two to three days. A 1–1.3 gallon bin forces this rhythm naturally. Do not buy a bigger bin to empty it less often — bigger means older scraps.
  2. Keep the lid closed and the filter fresh. Replace charcoal filters about every six months, or sooner if you notice a smell when the lid is closed.
  3. Line it or rinse it. Compostable bags make emptying clean; a quick rinse when you empty it keeps the bin itself from smelling. The Epica works best bagless with a rinse; the Joseph Joseph is designed for bags.

Then empty it into your system: a worm bin (see best worm bin for apartments), a bokashi bucket (see bokashi vs worm composting), or a compost drop-off. If you have no system yet, start with best compost bin for apartment to choose one.

What not to buy

  • A bin bigger than 1.75 gallons for a countertop. It will hold a week’s scraps, and a week’s scraps smell. Small and frequent beats big and rare.
  • A bin with no lid or no filter. Open bins attract fruit flies within 48 hours in a warm kitchen. A charcoal filter in the lid is non-negotiable.
  • An electric composter as a “countertop bin”. Machines like the Lomi 3 ($549) and Vitamix FoodCycler ($499) sit on the counter, but they are appliances, not scrap collectors — and their output still needs outdoor composting. They solve a different problem at twenty times the price.

FAQ

Do I need a countertop bin if I have a worm bin? Yes, for most people. The countertop bin is where scraps go while you cook; the worm bin is where they go every two or three days. Without the countertop bin, scraps go in the trash out of convenience.

How often should I replace the charcoal filter? About every six months, or sooner if the bin smells with the lid closed. Filters are cheap and sold in multi-packs.

Can I use regular plastic bags in a countertop bin? You can, but compostable bags are better — they go into the worm bin or compost system with the scraps. Regular plastic bags must be emptied and thrown away, which defeats the purpose.

Stainless steel or plastic? Steel does not stain or hold smells, and it looks better. Plastic is lighter, cheaper, and easier to clean if it has a smooth interior like the OXO. Either works — the filter and the emptying rhythm matter more than the material.
